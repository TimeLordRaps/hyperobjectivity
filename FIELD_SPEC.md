# Hyperobjectivity: object instantiation and invariance

Status: **[FRAME] finite research contract**. Here `[FORM]` would mean a proposition closed by a stated derivation in its named formal scope; `[FRAME]` is an explicit construction with unresolved obligations; `[OPEN]` is an unresolved question; `[HYPER]` is a proposed relation across recursive form levels. These tags do not themselves supply evidence. The objects in this specification are typed computational claims with no physical unit.

## Field boundary

Hyperstratum defines a **form** as identifiable structure and a **hyperform** as the closed or closing identity of a hyperstructure. Its short hyper-object sketch says an object's identity includes base appearance, meta-interpretations, recursive transformations, cross-level connections, whole-form role, and closure pressure. Tyler proposes the further relation: **a hyperobject is an instantiation of a hyperform**, and such objects can participate in subsequent hyperemergence. This repository takes the *instantiation and stable-object* question as its own. It does not redefine how a hyperstructure closes; Hyperstructure and Hyperstratum own that premise.

**[FRAME] Candidate definition:** Hyperobjectivity studies the conditions under which an instantiated hyperform can be treated as one hyperobject across admissible situated presentations, including which traits remain invariant and which differences belong to representation, frame, or subject. Objectivity here is a *bounded invariance claim*, not the assertion that every property is observer independent or that a named object exists outside the declared frame.

Formation dependency is recursive rather than a clock: forms contribute to a hyperstructure; a closure claim identifies a hyperform; an instantiation claim proposes a hyperobject; its interactions can contribute to a new hyperemergent and later hyperform. The latter need not be the earlier hyperform. The dependency index, if one is introduced, is not seconds or a physical time coordinate.

## Five distinct records in the finite witness

| Record | What it declares | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| `Form` | An identifiable source token | Native form admissibility |
| `HyperformClaim` | Source-form references and a closure-claim reference | That closure is valid |
| `HyperobjectClaim` | An object identifier bound to one claimed hyperform | That instantiation occurred |
| `Presentation` | Appearance, reality-kind label, and candidate traits for that object | That the presentation is exhaustive or veridical |
| `Transport` | A witness-claim reference relating two presentations of the same claimed object | That the witness is sound or transitive |

The `Registry` rejects dangling source forms, objects without claimed hyperforms, duplicate identifiers, duplicate trait names, and transports that switch the claimed object. It does not infer identity from equal appearances. Any two *different* presentations require an explicit declared transport for a continuity claim, even when they share a reality-kind label. That prevents a same-kind label or same object name from silently doing the work of an identity proof.

`invariant(object_id, trait)` compares **all registered presentations** of that claimed object. It reports `FAIL` when their stated values disagree; `UNKNOWN` with fewer than two presentations, missing trait evidence, or a missing pairwise transport; and `PASS` only for agreement under every declared pairwise transport in this finite registry. `PASS` means *finite contract satisfied under the declarations*, not native identity or metaphysical objectivity. Additional presentations or an invalidated transport can overturn it. The comparison is dimensionless unless an application separately supplies a measured quantity, unit, and observation procedure.

The pairwise requirement is deliberate. A path of transport claims is not automatically a sound composite transport, and a loop of presentations is not automatically a closed hyperform. A later compositional law may replace this conservative rule if it is independently justified and tested.

## Reality kinds and the subject seam

Hyperethics currently names `areality` (abstract artificial reality), `surreality` (dream reality), `preality` (possibility reality), and `base-reality` (universal base reality) as distinct, unranked kinds. This is a source taxonomy, not a geometric coordinate system. Tyler has now proposed **hypergeometric reality** alongside abstract, possibility, surreal, and base reality. The finite registry allows additional nonempty kind labels but does **not** assert that the proposed fifth kind is already defined, equivalent to the Hypergeometry field, or that five kinds exhaust the taxonomy. Its placement and cross-kind transports remain `[OPEN]`.

Hypersubjectivity may consume a hyperobject presentation *by explicit reference* to study an actual situated perspective. An object being presentable does not entail any subject experiences it; two subjects reporting the same trait do not establish its objective invariance. Conversely, different appearances do not by themselves split a claimed object when a sound continuity mechanism can account for them.

## Refutation and promotion gates

The tests include equal-looking different objects, a same-object claim without transport, contradictory trait values, missing evidence, and broken form ancestry. These are finite counterexamples to cheap identification rules. They cannot prove a native ontology because the registry's identifiers, source forms, closure references, and transports are supplied as data.

**[OPEN]** A native instantiation rule must define how an object comes from a hyperform without equating a representation with the object. A native closure criterion must establish when the source hyperstructure is a hyperform; a reference to a closure claim is insufficient. A sound transport calculus must say what is preserved across presentations and reality kinds, whether transports compose, and what a closed loop preserves. A cross-level observable and uncoupled counterfactual must distinguish later hyperemergence from a new name or edge. Physical applications additionally owe measured quantities, dimensions, uncertainty, and applicable balance laws. No such result is claimed by this repository.
